# Six practiced directions

Current artifacts: `x-content-engine/assets/covers/cover-lab-2026-10-07/round-2/`. Every family has a final PNG, exact JSON, 340 px preview and an original/candidate comparison. Six families cover half of the twelve editorial families identified in this particular corpus; no measured popularity ranking or CTR claim is implied.

## 1. Transparent dimensional glass — reference 06

**Use:** recognizable platforms/tools, distribution guides, a concrete relationship between entities.

**Structure:** huge dominant word; compact small lead-in; medium lower word; a shared foreground object group. All copy remains separate. Preserve readable central-letter overlap. The field provides a coherent light environment.

**Material:** the model renders real recognizable marks and glass together from the reference. The central face must transmit light/background; thick edges refract it. Do not insert an opaque App Store tile just to retain exact pixels. Inspect the logo after generation.

**Tested:** `final/01-glass.json` uses the paired asset `assets/01-glass-v1.png`. Round-1 v5 is a negative material example. New pair restores transparent bodies, colored refraction and natural relative perspective. Inspect `comparisons/01-glass.jpg` before adapting.

## 2. Philosophical monochrome engraving — reference 15

**Use:** agency, attention, choices, effort, hidden work, uncertainty. Can support a technical/business thesis through a clear indirect metaphor.

**Structure:** no mandatory headline; two or three large light/dark masses, one legible human action, dense engraved detail subordinate to silhouette. Avoid endlessly recycling a person falling into a vortex.

**Tested concept:** a person pulls aside a monumental curtain, revealing a horizon. The thesis is deliberate visibility. The giant scale is symbolic; hand/cloth/feet/ledge remain coherent. `final/02-monochrome.png` and its comparison show the image at feed size.

**Failure conditions:** scenic wallpaper without an action; tiny figure lost in stippling; random cosmic/cyberpunk objects; a metaphor that needs an essay to explain.

## 3. Warm editorial engraving — reference 17

**Use:** a production mechanism, systems, one person's useful leverage; not fabricated wealth.

**Structure:** cream paper, rust ink, materially appropriate serif/italic type, one detailed coherent machine/scene. An illustrated cutout can interact with the headline edge while retaining letter recognition. Warm print material does not require glossy glass objects.

**Tested:** one operator and one printing mechanism, copy “build once.” The generated cutout carries rust linework and cream-filled surfaces on alpha. `final/03-editorial.json` places separate Georgia type behind the foreground wheel. Machine anatomy and the final d/period were inspected.

**Failure conditions:** random gears, fake currency/results, busy details at equal contrast, ornate font chosen merely to look “premium”.

## 4. Cinematic physical metaphor — reference 19

**Use:** a tangible mechanism or short punchy thesis with an object that explains it.

**Structure:** dark coherent stage, low-key directional warm light, one large physical subject, condensed high-impact headline. Limit props; keep contact, scale and lighting believable.

**Tested:** “MAKE IT TRAVEL.” with a paper-plane sculpture resting on stone. Folded paper maps to designing work for distribution; it is not a photo of the author's actual object. A closer v2 still did not overlap letters. V3 crossed the lower T but its stone obscured the final L below. V4 retained the upper overlap and reduced the lower line enough to restore L. See retained iterations and `comparisons/04-cinematic.jpg`. This is why generic “move it closer” is not sufficient QA.

**Failure conditions:** generic cyberpunk city, floating cutout without a reason, glow replacing material, object unrelated to the thesis.

## 5. Tactile monochrome collage — reference 12

**Use:** human attention, ownership, fragmented media or another thesis that benefits from material contrast.

**Structure:** oversized serif word, torn paper/engraving, one high-contrast focal motif, compact supporting copy. Use an actual collage metaphor instead of copying banknotes and implied results from the source.

**Tested:** ordinary engraved eye on torn paper; copy “attention is earned.” V1 separated supporting copy too far; v2 enlarged the main word, raised the supporting line and balanced the strip. Full-size inspection then found overly tight t crossbars; v3 restored native whole-line kerning instead of per-glyph negative tracking. Typography remains separately editable.

**Failure conditions:** extra eyes/body parts, meaningless stamps, tiny fake newspaper writing, full-frame grunge that destroys hierarchy.

## 6. Technical route / blueprint — reference 20

**Use:** an actual sequence/system when its structure is the useful idea. Offer as a deliberately restrained route, not a fallback for a dramatic cover request.

**Structure:** one continuous route, few labeled nodes, exact vector-like geometry, quiet grid, one signal color. Simplify the original's density to survive feed size.

**Tested:** idea → format → audience. All lettering and geometry are deterministic. First pass put AUDIENCE on a bend and FORMAT too close to a connector. Second pass moved labels into clear space and increased their size; comparison reviewed at 340 px.

**Failure conditions:** decorative circuits, fake technical precision, label/line collisions, tiny essential text, invented metrics.

## Transfer to a new Article

Read the actual thesis, choose concepts first, then offer 2–3 fitting directions with original images and these practice outputs. Do not merely replace the words in the blue example. The reusable unit is the design decision and verified production method, not a universal layout.
