# Founder language — context first

Source: Kirill's vocabulary attachment and explicit instructions on 2026-10-03. These are open semantic families, not mandatory words or a new persona. Extend the family when the subject calls for it. Main voice and audience modes: [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md).

## Choose the job before the word

Use a term when it compresses a real mechanism, identifies a tradeoff, signals relevant experience or adds a natural reaction. If it only makes a simple thought look sophisticated, remove it. Familiar syntax can carry sophisticated ideas. Do not replace one exact metric with a fashionable near-synonym.

For broad AI/tech, start with a recognizable result or problem. Introduce specialist language after the reader understands why it matters. For a founder post, GTM, CAC, retention or payback can be the most direct wording. Do not automatically spell out every acronym for this audience. Explain only what the particular reader needs.

| Family | Useful vocabulary and nearby concepts | Use when the post actually concerns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information advantage | alpha, edge, signal, proprietary insight, asymmetry, arbitrage, gatekept, sauce | A specific information gap or advantage. Name the useful insight; do not label ordinary advice secret alpha. |
| Execution and stage | execution, 0→1, first win, ship, iterate, launch, validate, scale, bottleneck, feedback loop | Moving from an idea to something tested, or growing a process that already works. Keep first results separate from repeatability. |
| Distribution and GTM | GTM, distribution, acquisition, channel, organic, paid acquisition, inbound, cold outreach, funnel, conversion, ICP, positioning, wedge | How the product reaches a particular customer. Specify the channel, behavior or constraint behind the label. |
| Demand and fit | existing demand, demand capture, demand generation, PMF, CMF, pain point, use case, niche, willingness to pay | Whether someone wants the offer and whether a product or content format repeatedly serves that demand. CMF is useful practitioner shorthand, not a universal benchmark. |
| Business model and defensibility | bootstrapped, VC-backed, cash flow, recurring revenue, MRR, ARR, TAM, commodity, moat, pricing power, switching costs, owned audience | Funding, cash timing, revenue structure or a concrete reason the product is hard to replace. `Moat` needs a mechanism. |
| Unit economics | CAC, LTV, CPI, CPM, ROAS, ROI, gross margin, contribution margin, payback period, burn, runway | Costs, return, timing and survival. Specify what is being counted and over what period; avoid acronym chains with no decision attached. |
| Product and monetization | activation, retention, churn, cohort, onboarding, paywall, paid users, one-time purchasers, trial-to-paid, view-to-install | A specific step in the user journey. Say whether the outcome is a view, install, activation, purchase or recurring payment. |
| Mobile distribution | ASO, store listing, install intent, keyword ranking, localization, Tier 1 markets | App discovery and market choice. Name actual countries when they matter; `Tier 1` is contextual industry shorthand, not one universal list. |
| Attribution and measurement | attribution, attributed revenue, incremental lift, baseline, cohort, hourly deltas, correlation, conversion window | Where results came from and how confidently they can be assigned. Correlation is a clue, not proof of causality. |
| Content and creative work | UGC, AI UGC, creator, influencer, creative, hook, demo, hook-and-demo, format, skit, talking head, POV, slideshow, clipping, FYP | What is made, how it is distributed and what the viewer experiences. Avoid naming a whole production stack when one specific change is the point. |
| Repeatability and visual distribution | volume game, iteration speed, creative fatigue, winning format, creative testing, viral UI, remix, scroll-stopper, format-market fit | A repeatable learning or production process. Volume needs a feedback loop; a large single result does not establish repeatability. |
| Creator operations | base pay, milestone bonus, term, down payment, brief, outreach, in-house, contractor, coaching, delegation, high agency | Who owns the work, what they deliver, how they are paid and where judgment lives. Do not import someone else's operational tactics as policy. |
| Builder/AI shorthand | vibe-coded, wrapper, in prod, one-shot, slop, eval, inference, latency, context window, local-first | A real implementation or product distinction. A one-shot demo is not proof of a production-ready business. |
| Judgment and effort | high leverage, opportunity cost, first principles, tradeoff, compounding, diminishing returns, second-order effects | Why a particular action is worth doing. Show what multiplies, compounds or gets sacrificed. |

## Social texture

`ngl`, `btw`, `tbh`, `fr`, `rn`, `lol`, `wild`, `rough`, `brutal`, `cooked`, `cooking`, `cracked`, `slept on`, `mid`, `sold`, `I'm down`, `OG`, `goated`, `iykyk` are available when readable and appropriate. None is an ending template. Avoid obscure in-group shorthand for a broad audience and avoid turning slang into every sentence's payload.

Use `gatekept`, `sauce`, `crack the format`, `printing`, `cash-flow game` or `volume game` only with a concrete referent. `Printing` must not imply guaranteed income; `gatekept` must not invent exclusivity. Prefer a fresh observation to announcing that you are about to reveal a secret.

Do not borrow religion, identity, claimed relationships or personal history from the vocabulary's source author. `BH`, `thank GD` and similar expressions in the attachment describe that author's voice, not Kirill's beliefs. `Fake it till you make it` is not permission to fabricate credentials or results. Lists of tools are examples, not current endorsements.

## Preserve the distinctions

- MRR means recurring monthly revenue. A monthly revenue estimate, annual revenue, valuation and App Store gross sales are different quantities.
- Revenue is not profit. A 1.5x ROAS says revenue relative to ad spend; it does not establish profit after other costs.
- LTV and CAC must use compatible definitions. Payback and cash timing can matter even when a lifetime ratio looks attractive.
- Organic distribution can involve paid creators. `No paid ads` must not quietly become `free acquisition`, `no marketing budget` or `no costs`.
- Inbound describes how the lead arrives; it does not prove that acquiring the lead cost nothing.
- UGC can mean ordinary user content or paid creator-style assets. Name the relevant arrangement; an influencer's audience and a contractor's deliverable are different purchases.
- A conversion rate needs its denominator and window when they affect interpretation. Views→installs, installs→trials and trials→paid are not interchangeable.
- `Retention` needs a behavior, cohort or time window when used as a measured claim. A screenshot of revenue alone cannot prove PMF or CMF.
- `Scale` assumes something worth repeating. `TAM` cannot substitute for observed demand from a reachable customer.

## Calibration examples

These are hypothetical language examples, not Kirill's experience or reusable catchphrases. Use the actual input for every post.

| Bland draft | Sharper, specific direction |
| --- | --- |
| You need to think about marketing. | Your demo has a share button. Your GTM plan is hoping someone taps it. |
| Check if your marketing is working. | Which creative brought paid users? That's the attribution question I'd start with. |
| Execution is very important. | You've saved the playbook. Which part have you actually shipped? |
| This AI tool improves productivity. | Name the task it now completes, show the result, and say which frustrating step disappeared. |
| Scaling is important for apps. | Find the format that converts. Then decide how much you can spend repeating it. |

Do not use the sharper wording when it invents the reader's situation or Kirill's judgment. The lesson is to attach attitude to a specific action, object or tradeoff.
