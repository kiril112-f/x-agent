# EP's X Signal — operational content playbook

Source corpus: `x-content-engine/research/sources/eps-x-signal.md` by EP / `@eptwts`.

Это первый внешний playbook для content decisions. Он не выше явных инструкций Кирилла, его facts/privacy/brand voice и системных правил. Source содержит practitioner opinions, dated platform observations и grey-hat examples; внешние claims проверять отдельно.

## The content job

- Content is distribution. It should attract the people Kirill wants in his network and future product ecosystem.
- Every post must serve the reader: give a useful mechanism, decision rule, example, resource or perspective.
- Early-stage personal content earns attention when it turns real work into reader value. A diary entry without a useful lesson is not enough.
- Niche relevance beats random reach. Write for founders, builders, creators, marketers and AI operators, not for a generic viral audience.
- The account is a living proof-of-work and networking asset, not an ad-revenue project.

## Source of substance

Priority:

1. Kirill's real experience, draft, artifact, result, mistake or current decision.
2. Fresh primary information: official release, source code, dataset, product, original post/interview.
3. Proven content pattern from the swipe-file, adapted to Kirill's thesis.
4. Agent-generated connective language only after substance already exists.

AI may research, structure, format, compress and challenge. It must not manufacture the advice, experience or conviction.

## Simplicity and packaging

- The reader should understand the value in the first line.
- Use simple American English and concrete nouns. Preserve technical terms only when they do real work.
- Package around a clear benefit, result, tension or useful claim; do not bury the value behind setup.
- Hook and payload are one contract. A strong hook must be paid off by the post.
- Formatting is product UX: short semantic paragraphs, whitespace, scannable lists and no wall of text.
- One post carries one thesis. Extra good ideas become separate posts.

## No-fluff compression contract

The context/resource pack may be detailed. The final post is a lossy compression of that research: keep only what the reader needs to understand and remember the thesis.

Every sentence must perform at least one role:

- `Claim` — the post's main point.
- `Fact/proof` — sourced number, event, quote or artifact.
- `Mechanism` — why/how the claim happens.
- `Consequence` — what changes for the reader or market.
- `Example/resource` — concrete person, product, tool or case.
- `Decision/judgment` — Kirill's actual take or next move.

Delete a sentence when it only:

- repeats the previous sentence in new words;
- restates the hook after it is already clear;
- announces that the next point is important, surprising or new;
- adds a second speculative branch that is not needed for the thesis;
- lists every possible use case after one strong example already proves the mechanism;
- gives background that does not change the conclusion;
- summarizes the whole post again at the end;
- pads the post to look longer or smarter.

## Compression pass

1. Write the thesis in one sentence.
2. Mark the minimum fact/proof needed to support it.
3. Keep one clear mechanism.
4. Keep the strongest consequence or application.
5. Preserve one human line only if it adds voice rather than repetition.
6. Move optional resource links/details to a source note or suggested first reply.
7. Delete everything else.
8. Re-read: if removing a sentence leaves the meaning unchanged, remove it.

Do not optimize for the shortest possible text. Necessary nuance, attribution, caveats and story tension are not water. Compression must preserve meaning and Kirill's cadence.

## Authenticity and AI use

- Kirill supplies the core thought or approves an agent-found angle.
- AI formats and improves; Kirill remains author/editor.
- Slightly raw, clear writing is better than polished synthetic prose.
- Keep strong human sentences. Do not rewrite every line for uniformity.
- Record meaningful Kirill edits as `before → after → why` and update voice only from repeated/explicit feedback.
- DMs and real one-to-one interactions stay human/manual.

## Research and swipe-file

- Turn consumption into creation: a post, article, tool, launch or conversation can trigger a new angle.
- Build a swipe-file of hooks, structures and proof formats, not copied wording.
- Reverse-engineer accounts with repeatable performance, not a single viral outlier.
- Scrape narrowly, rank by relevance/performance, and return only the strongest ideas.
- Mention/credit the original person or resource when their work materially shapes the post.
- A source post proves what its author said, not every external claim inside it.

## Content types

- `Experience-based value`: real work → mechanism → reader application.
- `Build in public`: decision/artifact/problem → what changed → narrow lesson.
- `Case breakdown`: actor → constraint → action → result → mechanism.
- `Quote-post`: source supplies context; Kirill adds a new mechanism, example or judgment.
- `Resource post`: exact resource → what problem it solves → why it beats alternatives → who should use it.
- `Contrarian`: narrow falsifiable claim with evidence and a stated exception; no identity attack or ragebait.

## Promotion and trust

- Do not hard-sell by default. Educational value and proof build authority first.
- Integrate promotion only when the product/resource is the natural next step in the content.
- Never fabricate numbers, transformations, testimonials or income claims.
- High-quality lurkers, DMs, relationships and opportunities matter more than visible likes.
- Trust compounds; one misleading claim can cost more than a viral post earns.

## Replies and interaction drafts

- Write like a peer messaging a friend.
- Add a datapoint, mechanism, example, counter-case or real question.
- Never return generic praise or AI-sounding agreement.
- Keep replies concise, but use paragraph breaks when they contain more than one semantic beat.

## Freshness boundary

Re-verify before use:

- algorithm weights, ranking rules and posting-frequency claims;
- tool/model recommendations, prices and capabilities;
- platform payout benchmarks;
- any tactic marked dated, patched, grey-hat or based on one anecdote.

The durable writing principles may guide structure. Time-sensitive mechanics remain research hypotheses.
