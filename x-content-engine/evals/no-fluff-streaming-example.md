## What the example teaches

Keep the core claim, the named actor/model, the decisive number, and the new capability. Remove a second explanation of the same implication, speculative branches that do not change the conclusion, and transitions that merely say the news is important.

The user's preferred edit demonstrates compression, not a universal wall-of-text format. Project paragraph rules still apply.

## Compressed draft

The streaming industry just changed.

@fal post-trained the open-weight MiniMax H3 into H3 Max for better prompt following, visual quality, and speed.

It can generate 15 seconds of video with native audio in around 9 seconds. Faster than you can watch it.

## Sentence roles

1. Claim: the affected industry.
2. Fact/mechanism: who changed what and how.
3. Proof: the performance number and useful comparison.
4. Consequence: the concrete real-time streaming capability.
5. Judgment: the category shift.

- The extended Twitch/series/chat branching — interesting research context, but unnecessary in this compact post.
- A second restatement that content never ends — already implied by real-time next-scene rendering.
