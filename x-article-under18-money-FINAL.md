## 5 rules before you start anything

**1. Failures create the success.**

Everyone is waiting for the perfect plan with zero risk. Nobody has that plan. Edison burned through thousands of dead filament tests. James Dyson built 5,126 failed vacuum prototypes over 15 years of near-bankruptcy before number 5,127 worked. Michael Jordan got cut from his high school varsity team.

The experience is the asset. The win is a side effect.

**2. Money is a tool, not the goal.**

We all want cash for the Nikes or the new iPhone. Buy them, seriously, you'll remember it.

But the older you get, the more money turns into a number in an app sitting next to taxes, rent, food and people you're responsible for.

Under 18, while your mum is still cooking your dinner, you're in the one window of your life where you can *play* with money. Not in a casino. Invest it, reinvest it, put it into different things so that in ten years it comes back 1000x. Financial literacy built during puberty is an unfair advantage later.

**3. Your life is #1. The work is #2.**

You get one ticket, bro. There is way more happiness available to you than what's inside your boring projects. Build the thing, but go outside. You can't take money to the grave.

**4. Find what you actually like.**

Everything below is long-term and it works. But everyone is wired differently. Maybe you hate computers. Maybe you're an athlete. That's completely fine.

By the end of the day the only thing that matters is the one craft you mastered and enjoyed. Write your skills on paper and pick your own path.

**5. It's never too early and never too late.**

"That niche is dead." "AI took it." "I'm too young for this." That's your second voice talking bro, comfort zone excuses with a nice costume on. Just start.

## Social media platform monetization

[IMAGE]

X, YouTube, TikTok, Snapchat, Facebook and Instagram all pay creators out of their ad budget. Your attention is expensive right now, and platforms would rather pay you than lose you to a competitor.

I think it's the best place to start. You control how much you make, the barrier to entry is basically zero, and you walk out of it with skills that transfer everywhere: editing, storytelling, design, hooks, shipping on a schedule.

Platform payouts are also only one revenue line. Brand deals, native ads and promoting your own product all sit on top of the same audience.

MrBeast's version of this is still the best one:

> "All you need to do is make 100 videos and improve something every single time. Don't worry about views or subscribers on those first 100. Just focus on improving one thing each time. By the 101st video, you'll be in a completely different league."

**The actual requirements right now (Sept 2026):**

[IMAGE]

**YouTube** — 1,000 subscribers, plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days.

Pay attention to this one: on **February 1, 2027 those numbers double for new creators**, to 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views. If you're already in the Partner Program before that date, you keep the old numbers. So if you were planning to start "eventually", start now. The door gets twice as heavy in five months.

**TikTok Creator Rewards** — 10,000 followers + 100,000 views in the last 30 days, and your videos have to be over 1 minute to qualify. Only 8 countries are eligible: US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Brazil. RPM usually lands between $0.40 and $1.20 per 1,000 views, so this is a volume game, not a jackpot.

**X** — Creator Revenue Sharing shut down on September 7, 2026 and got replaced by the **Original Content Rewards Program**: X Premium + 500 verified followers + 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in 90 days. That impression bar is 10x lower than the old one, which is exactly why your feed is suddenly full of long articles like this one.

**Snapchat** — 50,000 followers + 15,000 hours of view time in the last 28 days, at least 3,000 of those from Spotlight, and you have to post 25+ times a month. It's invite-only. You can't apply.

**Facebook** — still invite-only. You submit interest in the Professional Dashboard and wait.

A lot of the rest comes down to your geo, your RPM and the small rules nobody reads. But a consistent $3k/month from this path is realistic if you actually make good content for a year. Not fast. Realistic.

**Read to the end for the 18+ part, because every single one of these programs is 18+.**

Pick your format, pick your style, pick a niche from something you genuinely watch, automate the boring parts with AI once you find the formula, then run it on multiple platforms.

## Campaigns (start here if you're 15)

[IMAGE]

Here companies pay you instead of the platform, and the rate is much higher. Usually around $1,000 per million views, sometimes up to $3,000.

The mechanic is simple: read the campaign rules, connect your accounts, make content that follows the brief, get paid per verified view.

**The formats:**

**UGC** — you and your hands, or your face, or an AI influencer. Anything you can film or generate.

**Slideshow** — a photo carousel with the call to action in the middle or at the end. Lowest effort format that still works.

**Clipping** — you find the good moments in someone's podcast or stream and cut them.

[IMAGE]

Because the pay is high and the barrier is near zero, this is where most people our age should start. Worst case you make pizza money on a Saturday. Best case you learn how brands actually think, which is real B2B experience at 15.

**Clipping & pay-per-view:**

• [Vyro](https://vyro.com/) — Beast Industries' clipping marketplace, fixed CPM on verified short-form views.
• [Clipify](https://discord.gg/clipify) — bot-powered clipping community, paid streamer and brand campaigns with automated view tracking.
• [ClipIt](https://discord.gg/clipit) — huge short-form community with open campaigns for artists, creators and entertainment brands.
• [Content Rewards](https://contentrewards.com/) — discovery hub for hundreds of creator bounty servers paying per view or per sale.
• [ClipFlip](https://clipflip.io/) — self-serve, fixed rates per million verified views on TikTok, Shorts and Reels.

**UGC & brand video:**

• [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/) — claim a product brief, film a short video, fixed fee.
• [Billo](https://billo.app/) — 15–30 second UGC video ads for e-commerce brands.
• [Brands Meet Creators](https://brandsmeetcreators.com/) — free daily briefs with transparent rates.

Two things decide whether you actually get paid. Read the brief like a contract, because most rejections are someone ignoring a rule about music, length or the exact hashtag. And sort out how the money reaches you before you start, since these pay through PayPal, Stripe or crypto, and all three are 18+ (last section covers that).

Pick one campaign, prepare your accounts, find the format that wins, then post it every day for 30 days and improve one thing each time.

## Personal brand

The most valuable one long-term, and the slowest.

AI writes a massive share of the internet now, and one person ships what a whole team used to. When everything is cheap to produce, authority is the thing that doesn't inflate. If you have a page with a real audience and a small product attached to it, you're already sitting on a business that will look like gold in 2030.

What it actually looks like day to day: pick a niche you could talk about for two years without getting bored, pick one platform, post on a schedule instead of when you feel inspired, and answer every comment and DM for the first year. The product comes after the audience, not before it.

Then you monetize through digital products, consulting or services.

## Freelance

Less attractive than the rest, I know. But if you master one specific job and you're actually good at it, you get clients. And AI makes you 3x faster at delivery, so you can take more work and eventually delegate it.

Honest warning first: Upwork and Fiverr are rough for a 15-year-old with zero reviews, because you're bidding against agencies with 500 finished jobs. Your realistic first client is a small creator or a Discord server that needs an editor this week. Do that one cheap, get the testimonial, then go compete on the big platforms.

• [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/) — biggest global marketplace, hourly or fixed contracts across tech, design, marketing and admin.
• [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/) — gig-based, you list fixed-price service packages.
• [Freelancer.com](https://www.freelancer.com/) — competitive bidding plus design and coding contests.
• [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/) — screened elite network, top 3% of devs, designers and finance people.
• [Contra](https://www.contra.com/) — commission-free portfolio and freelance platform.
• [PeoplePerHour](https://www.peopleperhour.com/) — bidding plus fixed-price offers, strong in web dev and content.
• [Guru](https://www.guru.com/) — flexible agreements with milestone, hourly or task-based invoicing.

One client first. Then a $10k/month agency, if you want it.

## SaaS

Every company, agency, creator and filmmaker on earth has a problem you can solve with software.

Building it used to require a team. Now it requires an idea, a coding agent and taste. Most people still have no clue what AI can actually do for their workflow, and that gap is your entire business.

The math is friendlier than it looks. A boring B2B tool at $30/month needs 100 customers to be $3k MRR. That's 100 people who have the problem, not 100,000 followers.

Find a problem, build the thing that solves it, market it, collect income for years.

## App development (my fav)

[IMAGE]

Five years ago you needed agencies, designers and software engineers. Now you need Claude Max, a laptop and like $1k for marketing.

You don't build the hard parts yourself either. RevenueCat handles the subscriptions, Supabase handles the backend, and a coding agent handles most of the rest.

Some genuinely stupid apps do $100k in revenue. Even $5k MRR is a big deal when your margin is 90%.

I'm probably making this sound easier than it is, so let me be honest: people are building billion-dollar companies with AI right now, and people are also shipping garbage that nobody downloads. Use AI as leverage on your thinking, not as a replacement for it.

Find an audience problem, build a simple app with good design, ship it to the App Store and Play Store, then make UGC content to promote it. That last step is the one everyone skips and it's the one that decides whether you make money.

## So how do you actually deal with 18+?

Straight answer: **every program above is 18+**, and there's one legitimate way through.

**Ask your parents.**

Google is the only one of these companies that documents it openly. If you're under 18, you monetize a YouTube channel by linking it to an AdSense account owned by a parent or guardian who is over 18. That's not a loophole, it's written in their own help docs. The catch is written there too: the payments go to the adult, not to you. YouTube doesn't care who filmed it, they care that they have a legal adult to pay.

The same logic covers the rest of the plumbing:

→ PayPal and Payoneer are 18+, but a parent can hold the account and receive on your behalf.
→ Revolut has an under-18 account for 6 to 17 year olds tied to a parent, and in some countries a 16-year-old can open one alone. Check what exists where you live.
→ Crypto exchanges like Bybit, tax forms, ID verification: same conversation.

It's a five minute talk that unlocks basically everything, and it goes a lot better if you show up with real numbers and a plan instead of "can you make me an account."

For TikTok, Snapchat, Meta and X there's no documented parent route. Their terms just require the account holder to be 18. So you either wait, or you build the audience now and monetize it the day you turn 18, which is what I'd do.

What I would **not** do:

Buying a verified account off someone. It's illegal, and every bit of responsibility lands on you.

And faking the birth date. It feels free until payout day, when identity verification runs against the account and the money and the channel both disappear. You did all that work for nothing.
